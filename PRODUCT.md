# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary reader is a **general reader discovering the epic** — someone curious about the Ramayana who wants an accessible, welcoming entry point rather than a scholar's reference. They arrive on a phone or laptop, often in a quiet moment, and the job is to *read, understand, and follow the narrative* verse-by-verse with Devanagari, transliteration, word-by-word meaning, and English translation in a calm, focused environment.

Alongside them: devotees who value reverence and continuity, and students/scholars who value legibility and precision. The interface must hold all three without centering any one — welcoming enough that a newcomer reads their first verse with understanding, dignified enough that a devotee feels respected, legible enough that a scholar can read for hours.

## Product Purpose

Bring the monumental Valmiki Ramayana translation — originally published at valmikiramayan.net (© 1998–2008) by Sri Desiraju Hanumanta Rao and Sri K. M. K. Murthy — into a contemporary, fast, accessible digital reading experience. 24,000 Sanskrit verses across 6 Kaandas and 534 sargas, each rendered in Devanagari alongside transliteration, word-by-word meaning, English translation, and commentary.

Success: a newcomer can open the site and read their first verse with both understanding and a sense of the sacred; a seasoned reader can navigate the full epic fluidly with good reading ergonomics. The site is a permanent, respectful home for a sacred text — not a product to be sold.

## Brand Personality

**Reverent, warm, timeless.**

Voice is calm, inviting, and unhurried — explanatory without being pedantic, sacred without being preachy. The emotional goal is the feeling of opening a treasured manuscript in a quiet temple hall: warmth, dignity, and welcome, not awe-at-a-distance. Heritage is conveyed through typography, color, and atmosphere, not through "ancient scroll" pastiche. Sacred, but inviting; heritage, without stuffiness.

## Anti-references

- **The legacy valmikiramayan.net** — cluttered, dated table-of-links Web-1.0 aesthetic this project was rebuilt to escape. Dense link indexes, no reading rhythm, no atmosphere.
- **Generic SaaS / startup landing** — cream cards, gradient hero metrics, corporate product-marketing feel. This is a sacred text, not a thing being sold; the page should never read as "selling" anything.
- **Over-ornate / wedding-invitation kitsch** — excessive gold filigree, heavy borders, clip-art motifs, skeuomorphic parchment scroll effects. Ornament that decorates the chrome cheapens the sacred; ornament must serve the verse.

## Design Principles

- **Reverence through clarity, not ornament.** The verse is the hero. Ornament serves the text — it never competes with it. When in doubt, subtract.
- **Welcome the newcomer, respect the devotee.** A discovery-first audience means the interface invites and explains without dumbing down the sacred content. Never make a first-time reader feel they need prerequisites.
- **Let the verse breathe.** Reading ergonomics are sacred. Legibility, rhythm, and focus on the verse come before decorative ambition. The reading surface is the product, not the chrome around it.
- **Timeless, not retro.** Heritage is carried by typography, color, and atmosphere — not by skeuomorphic "ancient manuscript" effects. Modern performance and accessibility are part of being timeless.
- **Restraint as a sign of respect.** The default move is to remove. Ornament earns its place only by enhancing the verse. Filigree that decorates the frame is the kitsch move; a single well-placed ornament is the reverent one.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**WCAG AA, plus strong reading ergonomics.** The verse reading surface is first-class:

- Devanagari Sanskrit must stay readable at all zoom levels and at the long-form reading sizes the content demands; the reading surface is tested, not assumed.
- Reduced motion is honored everywhere (scroll-reveal, glyph-draw, and progress motion all have instant/crossfade alternatives).
- Full keyboard navigation; the search, kanda dropdown, and verse navigation are operable without a pointer.
- Screen-reader-friendly verse structure: Sanskrit, transliteration, word-by-word meaning, and translation are distinct, navigable regions — not a flattened blob.
- Body and verse text meet contrast targets; muted/placeholder text does not fall below 4.5:1. The reading surface avoids the muted-gray-on-tinted-near-white failure.
